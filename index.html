My undergraduate thesis, "Multimodal AI for Early Detection of Parkinson’s Disease," was supervised by Prof. AHM Ashfak Habib, investigating multimodal deep learning and transformer-based fusion across clinical biomedical data with explainable AI (SHAP & Grad-CAM).

CUET_320@LT-EDI-2025: A Multimodal Approach for Misogyny Meme Detection in Chinese Social Media

M. A. Chowdhury, Lamia Tasnim Khan, M. S. Hasan, A. Dey
Proceedings of the 5th Conference on Language, Data and Knowledge (LT-EDI Workshop), 2025
Ranked 1st place globally in the international shared task. Formulated a multi-stream multimodal deep learning architecture fusing visual affective semantics with multilingual language models, utilizing cross-attention gating to detect subtle misogynistic rhetoric where benign text masks aggressive imagery.
Google Scholar Record
Abstract: Online social media platforms are inundated with multimodal memes where derogatory and misogynistic commentary is deliberately concealed behind metaphorical imagery, colloquial slang, and cultural irony. In this paper, team CUET_320 details our winning system for the LT-EDI@LDK 2025 International Shared Task on Misogyny Meme Detection in Chinese Social Media. We construct a multi-stream representation network combining vision transformers with contextualized multilingual language encoders. By introducing a cross-attention fusion layer that explicitly models semantic dissonance between visual and linguistic modalities, our system achieved first place among all international competing teams on macro F1-score and accuracy.

CUET320 at SemEval-2026 Task 10: Few-Shot Large Language Models for Psycholinguistic Marker Extraction and Conspiracy Detection

F. Fariha, Lamia Khan, M. A. Chowdhury, K. Ahmed, M. M. Hoque
Proceedings of the 20th International Workshop on Semantic Evaluation (SemEval-2026), 2026
Investigated few-shot in-context learning with domain-adapted LLMs to extract fine-grained psycholinguistic markers (epistemic certainty, agency, emotional valence) and accurately identify conspiracy narratives across complex multi-domain social discourse.
Google Scholar Record
Abstract: Conspiracy discourse relies heavily on subtle psycholinguistic manipulation rather than overt falsehoods alone. In SemEval-2026 Task 10, we evaluate instruction-tuned and open-weights Large Language Models under constrained few-shot prompting setups to extract latent psycholinguistic dimensions. We demonstrate that structured chain-of-thought elicitation focused on epistemic certainty and attribution markers outperforms conventional fine-tuned encoder baselines, yielding robust generalization across unseen conspiracy topics without overfitting to superficial lexical triggers.

CUETClashing at SemEval-2026 Task 1: Multilingual Joke Generation Under Lexical and Topical Constraints Using Small Instruction-Tuned LLMs

M. A. Chowdhury, Lamia Khan, F. Fariha, S. H. Shohan, M. M. Hoque
Proceedings of the 20th International Workshop on Semantic Evaluation (SemEval-2026), 2026
Engineered constrained generation pipelines leveraging efficient, small instruction-tuned language models (SLMs) to achieve strict dual lexical compliance and topical fidelity in cross-lingual humor generation without relying on massive proprietary models.
Google Scholar Record
Abstract: Computational humor generation under rigid lexical and thematic constraints represents a severe challenge for neural generation models, which typically sacrifice humorous timing for constraint satisfaction or vice-versa. We investigate parameter-efficient instruction-tuned small language models (SLMs) for SemEval-2026 Task 1. By combining structured constrained beam filtering with iterative self-critique prompts, our pipeline achieves 100% lexical constraint preservation across multiple languages while maintaining strong ratings in semantic surprise and comedic payoff.
