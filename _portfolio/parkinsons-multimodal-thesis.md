### Abstract & Overview

Parkinson’s Disease (PD) is an insidious neurodegenerative condition that degrades motor and non-motor neural pathways. Early clinical intervention substantially improves patient outcomes, but early screening is often hindered by subjective clinical evaluations and heterogeneous patient symptom manifestations.

In this thesis, we investigate multimodal deep learning approaches for early Parkinson’s disease detection using heterogeneous clinical and biomedical data:
- **Modality-Specific Encoders:** Designed deep learning encoders to extract dense latent representations from heterogeneous biomedical signals.
- **Transformer-Based Multimodal Fusion:** Formulated cross-attention fusion strategies enabling mutual information exchange across modalities prior to classification.
- **Model Interpretability (XAI):** Integrated SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) and Grad-CAM for improving model interpretability, providing transparent diagnostic rationales.
