## Overview & Motivation

Conspiracy theories propagate through distinct psycholinguistic structures—characterized by hyper-certain epistemic framing, agency attribution, and specific affective markers. Detecting these structures automatically without succumbing to lexical over-fitting is a central open challenge in computational sociolinguistics.

In this paper, our team (**CUET320**) developed a few-shot large language model methodology for **SemEval-2026 Task 10** to isolate latent psycholinguistic dimensions and predict conspiracy tendencies in complex narrative discourses.

## Technical Highlights

- **Few-Shot In-Context Framing:** Formulated structured prompt templates with chain-of-thought psycholinguistic scaffolding, allowing the LLM to inspect narrative framing before generating classification decisions.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Evaluated open-weights and instruction-tuned LLMs under constrained parameter regimes, benchmarking against conventional transformer classifiers.
- **Error Analysis & Interpretability:** Conducted fine-grained error analysis to demonstrate how distinct markers (e.g., certainty adverbs, external threat lexicons) correlate with conspiracy detection accuracy.
